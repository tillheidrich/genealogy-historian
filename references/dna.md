# DNA tests in family history

## What a test can and cannot show

- **Ethnicity estimates** are statistical models built on reference panels. They change with every update and say little about individual ancestors. Treat them as level H context at most, never as evidence for a specific person or place.
- **Matches** (relatives who share DNA with the tester) and the **amount of shared DNA** in centimorgans (cM) are the usable part.
- DNA never names an ancestor by itself. It supports or contradicts a relationship that paper records suggest.

## Reading shared cM

- Always give a range of possible relationships for a cM value, never a single one. Half relationships overlap with full relationships one step further away (a half sibling shares about as much as a grandparent, aunt or uncle).
- Use the Shared cM Project tables (DNA Painter) as orientation. They are based on user submissions and show ranges, not certainties.
- Providers measure differently (for example how small segments are treated, or whether the X chromosome is counted). Compare values from the same provider, or note the difference.
- Decide a relationship only together with the paper trail: ages, generations, places, and shared matches (who else matches both people).

## Privacy and consent

- A DNA test reveals information about relatives who did not consent. Ask before you upload kits or name matches.
- Companies can be sold or go bankrupt (23andMe, 2025), and their data with them. Tell the user how to download and delete data.
- Unexpected results (unknown parentage, donor conception, adoption) affect living people. Report them carefully and privately, and do not publish them.

## Recording

Store for each match: provider, kit owner (only with consent), shared cM and segments, predicted relationships, the relationship you finally assign, and the paper evidence for it.
