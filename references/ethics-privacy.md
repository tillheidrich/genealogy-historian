# Ethics, privacy and rights

## 1. Living people

- Never publish addresses, phone numbers or e-mail addresses of living people. This also applies to private, access-controlled family sites: links get forwarded, and access control fails. Give the town, not the street, and round map coordinates to the town.
- Living adults: by default only the year of birth and of marriage. Full dates only with the person's consent.
- Minors: year of birth only, no photos without the parents' consent.
- Health, pregnancy, religion, political views or voting, sexual orientation, criminal records of living people: leave out, also on private sites, unless the person has agreed. For people who died within the last 30 years, ask the closest relatives first.
- Do not upload scans or documents with data of living people to public forums or AI services without the consent of those people.
- Ask before you name living relatives publicly. A private, access-controlled site is different from a public one; say which one a page is.
- Respect the legal closure periods for civil registers (births 110, marriages 80, deaths 30 years in Germany).

## 2. The dead and their families

- Sensitive facts about the dead (illegitimacy, suicide, crimes, illness, Nazi-era membership) affect living descendants. Record them accurately; decide with the family what is published and how.
- Never hide or soften documented facts in the research data. Publication can be selective; the record must not be falsified.

## 3. Rights

- Photos and letters from family collections: the family usually owns the objects, but copyright may lie with the photographer or author (in Germany 70 years after the author's death; simple photographs 50 years after publication or creation). Credit studios when known.
- Archive scans: check the archive's terms. Many allow private use and require permission for publication. Link instead of copying when unsure.
- Database content (indexes, edited card files): cite and link; do not mirror.
- Embedding (hotlinking) a scan from another server is a kind of copying. Link by default; embed only when the user decides so, and record that decision and the provider's terms.
- If a connector or database asks you to pass on a notice (for example on data quality), show it where its data is used, also on the published page.
- Public-domain material (for example museum open access images): credit the institution as a courtesy.

## 4. Access and security

- Never enter passwords, payment data or identity numbers on behalf of the user.
- Never bypass paywalls, bot protection or rate limits. Ask the user to pass checks in their own browser.
- Do not put secrets (tokens, access links) into public documents or repositories.

## 5. Honesty with the user

- Say clearly when a result is uncertain, when a search failed, and when a story does not hold up.
- Do not overstate what AI tools can do. OCR and handwriting recognition need human checking; models can misread and invent.
