# Ancestors in the Nazi era

Research into ancestors between 1933 and 1945 touches guilt, victimhood and family loyalty. Be exact, neutral and complete.

## 1. Principles

- Report what the records show, in neutral language. No verdicts, no excuses.
- Membership in the NSDAP or an affiliated organisation proves membership, not specific actions or convictions. Say so.
- Non-membership or a missing card proves nothing either: about 20 percent of the NSDAP cards are lost, and many people supported the regime without joining.
- Always consider the perspective of victims: persecution, forced labour, prisoners of war in the area, deportations from the place where the family lived.
- Children and teenagers of the time lived inside the system; describe their membership in youth organisations in that context.

## 2. NSDAP member card file

- Holding: Bundesarchiv R 9361-VIII (central card file) and R 9361-IX (regional card file).
- A searchable edition exists (for example the SPIEGEL NSDAP archive, also as a connector for AI assistants). Follow its notices. If it asks you to show a notice once, show it once, verbatim.
- Search with name, birth date, birthplace and residence. OCR often misreads names (J read as F, M as K); verify every hit on the scan.
- Read on the card: name, occupation, birth date and place, membership number, application date (Antrag), admission date (Aufnahme), address, local group, later changes.
- Interpret dates in context:
  - Admission stop from 1 May 1933. Many who applied in spring 1933 all received 1 May 1933.
  - Reopening on 1 May 1937, at first for selected groups; general from 1939.
  - Admission dates were set to the first of a month, from 1942 often to 20 April. The application date says more about the person's decision than the admission date.
  - From early 1942 admissions mainly came from the Hitler Youth and League of German Girls, often at 17 or 18.
  - Historians agree that nobody became a member without their knowledge and that there was no direct compulsion, but social and professional pressure.
- Consecutive membership numbers and identical application dates of relatives show joint applications. State that as an observation.
- Link scans; do not embed or republish them unless the terms allow it. Cite passages from the provider's background articles with title and link.

## 3. Wehrmacht, prisoners, losses

- Bundesarchiv Abteilung PA: service, units, wounds, captivity, release. Requests by relatives; give full name, birth date and place, and what is known.
- Volksbund grave search for the fallen.
- Family stories about the front or captivity ("near Paris, American captivity, nearly shot") are hypotheses to test against PA records, not facts.

## 4. Persecution

- Arolsen Archives online, memorial books (for example the Bundesarchiv Gedenkbuch for Jewish victims), local memorial projects (Stolpersteine).
- Forced labour and prisoner camps near the family's home: local archives, Arolsen, company records.

## 5. Writing

- Put the record first, then the context with a citation, then what remains open.
- Example: "Application 1 June 1943, admission 1 July 1944, aged 24 (A, NSDAP central card). From 1942 the party admitted mainly young people from the Hitler Youth; why he applied is not documented."
- Do not speculate about motives. If the family knows something, mark it E.


## Insinuation

Do not raise questions of guilt or involvement only because a person belonged to a group (for example "the camp guards were disabled miners, and he was a disabled miner"). Without an individual indication this is an insinuation. Describe the context on a context page; on the person's page say only what sources say about that person.
