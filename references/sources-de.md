# German and Central European sources

Check availability and access rules on the archive's own site before you promise a result. Holdings, digitisation and fees change.

## 1. Civil registers (Standesamt)

- Prussia from 1 October 1874, the whole German Reich from 1 January 1876. Birth, marriage and death registers (Geburts-, Heirats-, Sterberegister), each with a second copy (Nebenregister).
- Left bank of the Rhine (Rhineland, Palatinate, parts of Hesse): French civil registration (Zivilstandsregister, état civil) from 1798 until 1875, often with ten-year indexes (tables décennales).
- Where they are:
  - Within the closure period: the local Standesamt of the place of the event (today often merged into a larger municipality).
  - After the closure period: the municipal archive (Stadtarchiv) or the state archive. In NRW many second copies are at the Landesarchiv NRW (Abteilung Rheinland, Duisburg; Abteilung Westfalen, Münster), holdings named "Personenstandsregister" (PA). Some are digitised on the archive portal archive.nrw.de; many are not. Check the specific holding before you say a register is online.
- Closure periods under the Personenstandsgesetz (§ 5 PStG): births 110 years, marriages 80 years, deaths 30 years. Inside the period, access needs a legal interest or direct descent; descendants usually qualify with proof of relationship.
- Marginal notes (Randvermerke) record later marriages, deaths, legitimations and name changes. Read them.

## 2. Church books (Kirchenbücher)

- Before civil registration the church books are the main source: baptisms, marriages, burials, sometimes confirmations and family registers (Familienbuch, Seelenregister).
- Protestant books: many digitised on Archion (paid). Regional church archives answer requests.
- Catholic books: many dioceses on Matricula (free), others in diocesan archives.
- Duplicates (Kirchenbuchduplikate) were often filed with courts; in Prussia from 1794. They may survive when originals are lost.
- Former eastern territories: books may be in Polish state archives, Polish diocesan archives, the Evangelisches Zentralarchiv Berlin, or lost. Check before you write a request.

## 3. Family books and local studies

- Ortsfamilienbücher (OFB) compile families of a parish; many are listed on GenWiki and online-ofb.de. Treat them as derivative and check key entries against the books.
- Local chronicles, school chronicles, Heimatbücher: leads, not proof.

## 4. FamilySearch

- Large index collections ("Germany, Births and Baptisms", "Germany, Marriages") and microfilmed registers.
- Many German images are locked for home users and viewable only in a FamilySearch Center or affiliate library. Say so instead of promising the image.
- The catalogue (search by place) shows which films exist even when images are locked.

## 5. National archives and specialised holdings

- **Bundesarchiv**:
  - NSDAP member card file R 9361-VIII (central) and R 9361-IX (regional), originals in Berlin-Lichterfelde; about 80 percent survive.
  - Abteilung PA (formerly Deutsche Dienststelle / WASt): records of the Wehrmacht, losses, prisoners of war in Allied hands. Relatives can request information.
  - Many other personal files (Reichsarbeitsdienst, party correspondence). See ns-era.md.
- **Volksbund Deutsche Kriegsgräberfürsorge**: grave search for soldiers who died in both world wars.
- **Arolsen Archives**: documents of Nazi persecution, forced labour and displaced persons, online archive. Link and cite documents; do not republish copies without checking the terms.
- **Verlustlisten 1914–1918**: Prussian and other casualty lists, searchable at des.genealogy.net (Verein für Computergenealogie). Entries include wounded, missing and prisoners, often with birthplace.

## 6. Newspapers and print

- **Deutsches Zeitungsportal** of the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek: full text of many historical newspapers; an API allows proximity searches of name and place. See newspapers-ocr.md.
- State and university libraries run their own portals (for example digital collections of ULB Halle, BLB Karlsruhe, Bavarian State Library digipress). Some protect page images with bot checks; ask the user to open them in their own browser.
- **Address books (Adressbücher)**: city directories list household heads, occupation and address, often with the owner of the house. Many are digitised by state libraries and GenWiki lists them. Women appear mostly as widows or as owners.
- **Official gazettes** (Amtsblätter, Reichsanzeiger): appointments, awards, court notices, bankruptcies.

## 7. Poland, Silesia, Pomerania, East Prussia

- Many German-era civil registers and church books are in Polish state archives (Archiwum Państwowe), for example in Wrocław, Jelenia Góra, Legnica, Opole, Szczecin, Olsztyn.
- **szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl**: catalogue and scans of Polish archives. Search by today's place name and by the holding "Urząd Stanu Cywilnego w ...". The portal is sometimes unavailable; note it and retry later.
- **Geneteka** (Polish Genealogical Society): free indexes of parish and civil records; coverage of German-era Lower Silesia is patchy. Search with umlauts and variants.
- **Place names**: use a concordance (for example ehemalige-ostgebiete.de, GOV of genealogy.net, Kartenmeister) to get the Polish name, the parish and the civil registry district. A village often had no church or registry of its own.
- Write to Polish archives in Polish or German or English; many answer in Polish. Give today's and the historical place name.

## 8. Czech Republic, Austria, other

- Czech regional archives have digitised most church books (for example portafontium.eu for Western Bohemia, actapublica for Southern Moravia).
- Austria: Matricula for many Catholic dioceses; ANNO (Austrian National Library) for newspapers.

## 9. Emigration

- Hamburg passenger lists 1850–1934 (Staatsarchiv Hamburg, indexed on Ancestry), Bremen passenger lists (passagierlisten.de, partial), American arrival records (Ellis Island / Statue of Liberty foundation, FamilySearch).

## 10. Practical rules

- Find the jurisdiction at the time of the event: parish, civil registry district, county, state.
- Find where the records are today, and in which form (original, copy, film, scan, index).
- Note fees and waiting times in your recommendation.
- Never claim a register is online until you have seen the scan list for that holding.
